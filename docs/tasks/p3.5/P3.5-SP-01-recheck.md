## Blocking

### B01 — A matching subject still turns hearsay into direct evidence

**Location.** DL §4.3, MF §3.5 and MF-24, MM §§4.1–4.2, ADR 0011 D4.

**Conflict.** MM requires the resident to see for itself before adding
an observed source to a told claim. MF checks only whether the model's
record and the delivered entry share a subject key. MF-24 expressly
treats the presence of the spring tile as grounds to lift the confidence
cap for the poison claim. The fix tests topical overlap without testing
what the observation actually supports.

**Counterexample.** B hears “the spring is poisoned.” Later, B sees the
spring tile. Its packet reports the coordinates and terrain, with no
descriptor or phenomenon about contamination. The change set cites the
heard utterance as told and the sighting as observed. Both citations
pass MF-24, and the shared place key lets the observed citation lift
the cap to 4. B has observed the spring's location. The
claim about poison still comes entirely from speech.

**Minimum correction.** Bind an observed source to the specific fact the
resident's delivery witnessed, such as location, an apparent descriptor
or an observed outcome. Require a typed match for the claim being given
observed provenance. Keep extrapolations as told or inferred and retain
their original sources. Split MF-24 into a negative same-subject case
with an unrelated attribute, and a positive case in which the delivered
evidence supports the specific claim. These are attribution checks over
delivered data, with no read of hidden world truth.

### B02 — The new witness rule leaves mechanical commitment sources undefined

**Location.** MM §§3.5 and 4.1, MM-17, MF §§1.1 and 3.5, DL §4.3.
This conflict was introduced by applying the new witness requirement
in the common source definition.

**Conflict.** MM §3.5 and MM-17 require the code-written commitment to
retain an observed source for its owner's own proposal or acceptance
when the corresponding feedback is present. MM §4.1 now applies the
subject-witness requirement to an observed source generally. MF §3.5's
subject-key mapping omits commitment records and disallows observed
sources for records with no keys. DL §4.3 also supplies no specific
witness key for the act of speaking that the commitment source records.

**Counterexample.** A receives successful feedback on its own proposal,
then sleeps through B's acceptance. MM-17 requires A's mechanical record
to cite the proposal feedback as observed and to have no told source for
the missed acceptance. Applying the common witness rule makes that
observed source ineligible, so the two clauses give different expected
records even though the packets are identical.

**Minimum correction.** State the scope of the subject test and specify
how the code-written own-utterance witness satisfies it, or explicitly
keep that typed mechanical witness on its existing packet-verified path.
Preserve its narrow meaning of “I spoke.” Test a delivered own proposal
and an unheard counterpart response together, with no source obtained
from a private event ID.

## Non-blocking

### N01 — The transfer example omits the new split cause

**Location.** EN §§6 and 11, EN-17 and EN-36.

A partial kernel transfer in §6 still lists only `from_id`, `new_id` and
`quantity` on `stack_split`. Section 11 now requires `cause`. A partial
give should emit `cause: "transfer"`, while EN-36's reserved portion
emits `cause: "reservation"`. The canonical §11 schema determines the
field and values, so add the tag to the §6 example and assert it in
EN-17.
