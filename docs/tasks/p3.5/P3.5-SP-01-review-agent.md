## Blocking findings

### B01 — The bilateral fact feed manufactures heard provenance

**Location.** MF §§1–1.1, 3.3 and MF-01–02. MM §§0.3, 3.3, 4.1 and
6. DL §4.2 and DL-07. WAP §§5.1 and 7.2, PR-D5. ADR 0011 D4/D6 and
ADR 0012 D1.

**Problem.** PR-D5 requires both mechanical commitment records. MF-D1
routes objective events only to a writer, but MF labels the other party's
utterance `told` even without a delivery. It then allows the records into
prefix, recall and consolidation. The index gist alone can carry an unseen
fact. MF-02's request-hash assertion conflicts with that route, and an
unqualified export of mechanical writes to the change-set call bypasses the
memory projection as well.

**Counterexample.** A proposes giving a named axe, falls asleep, and B accepts
within hearing range. A gets the required record and no `HeardUtterance`.
The draft lets that record cite the unheard acceptance as `told`; EV can
mistake it for evidence of hearing. A sleeping recipient can likewise get
a transfer counter without a `give` observation.

**Fix.** Specify an allowlisted party-to-writer projection, its immutable
mechanical provenance and the code-only event linkage. Define precisely which
normalized obligation or transfer fields may enter the on-arm model view under
PR-D5 and MF-D1. Reserve `told` for a real, recipient-specific delivery.
Test both parties, a non-party, both arms, and direct and indirect model
surfaces. Check the allowlisted projection separately from the raw feed.

### B02 — Fallback results can contaminate resident knowledge and emergence evidence

**Location.** DL §§4.3–4.4, 8.3 and DL-14. MF §§1–1.2. EV §§0.2, 3.2–3.3.
ADR 0013 D7, ADR 0014 D2 and WAP §11.2/WAP-25.

**Problem.** DL excludes an adopted fallback response but its day-log templates
still consume each delivered action or utterance result. MF counts each
matching ended act and preserves mechanical writes. The rules do not follow
fallback origin to results delivered after the model returns. EV rejects a
fallback decision as a counted success but fails to exclude it as a hop or
learning event source.

**Counterexample.** A fallback gather starts on one turn, fails when the tree
empties, and the next model request renders its failed feedback or later
recalls a skill whose evidence was updated by it. A fallback utterance with
an otherwise eligible citation path can also seed an automatic chain through
a different resident.

**Fix.** Carry origin by intent and turn ID through delivery, log assembly,
consolidation and evaluation. Filter the actor's fallback actions and
feedback before model-facing or subjective writes. Preserve objective records,
valid perception of resulting state, and required mechanical party records
under their own contract. Preserve genuine observations by other residents
in their context, and specify the fallback exclusion when attributing
discovery, teaching or acted-on evidence. Test a delayed stop, a multi-tick
completion and a restart across the model → fallback → model transition.

### B03 — The validator can cite evidence never supplied to the model

**Location.** MF §§3.2–3.5 and 4. MM §§4.1 and 5–6. ADR 0011 D4/D6
and ADR 0012 D2.

**Problem.** The provenance check names the working context, pending periods
and index as broad membership sets. It does not bind source kinds and
operands to the exact post-guard input or distinguish an index ID from a
full record the model can revise. There is no explicit registry check for
the speaker, inferred call and parents, or code-only `innate` origin.
Pending experience is allowed at the next consolidation under ADR 0012 D5.
Its authorized read needs to be distinct from forbidden retrieval from audit.

**Counterexample.** A guarded change-set call never sees entry E. An
`add` guesses E's predictable ID and cites it as observed. An `update`
also rewrites an old belief the index listed but the model did not select.
A claim attributed to a trusted speaker can cite the ID of a different
speaker's real utterance.

**Fix.** Build a typed allowlist from the actual change-set input. Resolve
`observed` against direct perceptions, `told` against the delivered
utterance and matching speaker, and `inferred` against the pinned call and
allowed parent versions. Reserve `innate` for initialization. Define
allowed index-only references and require full authorized records for
mutating existing records. Provide a resident-local, consolidation-only
pending input. Test guessed omissions, wrong speaker, forged call/innate
sources and an attempt to retrieve a forgotten record.

### B04 — Closed operation names do not protect the hard facts

**Location.** MF §§3.3–3.5 and MF-06–07. MM §§3, 5 and 7, MM-10 and
MM-12. ADR 0011 D5/D7 and ADR 0012 D1–D2.

**Problem.** `NewRecord.content` is an open route into kinds containing
code-owned fields. The prose restricts `update` but fails to define
creation, merge, supersession and eviction permissions for those kinds.
Directly forgetting skill evidence is refused, but capacity eviction of
refuted records and history pruning have no consistent preservation rule.
A hypothesis can also change while keeping counters accumulated for the
old prediction, and the per-operation trait rule permits two one-step
writes to the same trait in one consolidation.

**Counterexample.** A syntactically valid `add` creates a commitment for a
fictional agreement, or a `merge` of two skills writes a new 10/10 evidence
counter. Changing a hypothesis from “snaps” to “holds” leaves prior matches
attached to the opposite prediction. Two trait updates of +1 take a trait
starting below base to above base in one consolidation.

**Fix.** Specify a kind-by-operation and field-authority matrix, including
automatic edits, history, archive and the extension registry. Make hard
fact creation and counters code-owned. Give historical skill evidence an
immutable association with its hypothesis revision and define model versus
inspection visibility after eviction. Enforce at most one authorized step
per trait per consolidation and validate all hard-fact paths, including
indirect removal by supersession and capacity.

### B05 — Transitive rejection lacks a transaction model

**Location.** MF §§3.3 and 3.5, MF-07. MM §§5 and 7. ADR 0012 D2/D5.

**Problem.** The dependency definition covers a created ref and records named
by merge, supersede or forget. It omits dependence on an earlier update, on
temporary state used in validation, and on automatic evictions. It also
leaves the handling of a later-failing producer and side effects of failed
operations ambiguous.

**Counterexample.** An invalid update is intended to add an observed source
to a told-only record. A later confidence operation raises it to 4, and a
third op uses that revised record as an inferred source. The third op can
survive using the old record because the declared dependency rules omit the
update. An overflowing belief add can evict an unrelated belief if the
eviction runs before a later check drops that add.

**Fix.** Pin reference resolution and dependencies over record revisions and
declared effects, including implicit mutations. Validate and stage each op
atomically against the accepted prefix or define an equivalent deterministic
transaction algorithm. Publish only accepted effects. Record stable reason
codes and dependency IDs. Test invalid producers, transitive dependents,
capacity side effects and an independent valid op in one set.

### B06 — The store-version exception does not define a safe commit at K

**Location.** MM §§1 and 8. MF §§1, 3.1, 3.5–3.6, 5 and 7, MF-08/13.
DL §§6.1 and 10.3. ADR 0012 D2/D5.

**Problem.** MF waives version checks for mechanical writes on the premise
that they touch only fields the model cannot change. A model update and a
fact write can share one record and its metadata. Writes can also change
what the model would have been required to see. Use counters and intention
writes have no explicit place in the version taxonomy. The pending policy
does not say which periods are consumed when a guarded retry succeeds.

**Counterexample.** While a social-impression update is outstanding, a give
updates the same record and an acceptance creates an open commitment. At
`s + K`, replay and live execution can disagree on the social record's
metadata, whether to process a new mandatory record, and which write
preceded the hash. After an earlier failure, selection can see pending P
but the change-set guard omits it. The next attempt can either reuse P or
silently lose it under the current wording.

**Fix.** Define a pinned snapshot, a field-level rebase onto the ordered
latest version, conflict rules and a cutoff for mandatory inputs. Pin the
order for writes at `s + K`, version/hash semantics for metadata and use
counters, and audit the accepted operations plus their committed effects.
For each pending period, specify which executed call consumes it and record
included, omitted, retained and evicted states. Verify identical results
with a restore between selection and commit.

### B07 — The replay formats do not specify the ingredients of every request

**Location.** DL §§4.2, 6.3 and 10.1–10.3, DL-16. MF §§3.2–3.3 and 7.
EV §§1 and 6. ADR 0013 D4/D10. P3
[LLM decision spec](../../spec/llm-decision-v0.1.md) §§2.2 and 4.3.

**Problem.** DL's manifest refs do not define how to retain several
call-specific inputs, including the exact recall query, structured schemas
and framing per call, guard notice, projected mechanical writes, and
runtime-derived current state. The decision record does not cover a morning
call with no adoption. The record model is unclear about per-attempt joins
and which P3 exact-request values it inherits versus replaces. A pressure
entry with the counted total and cue text cannot reconstruct the omitted
parts or the pressure-free framing.

**Counterexample.** A deliberate recall follows a guarded decision request.
The adopted turn output is an `act`. Its turn record alone cannot recover
the query, so the call output needs an explicit join. The sent manifest
references only the sent parts. Without the ordered pre-guard assembly,
its stored band and hash cannot establish what the pressure counter saw.
The same gaps show up in a failed morning call and an abandoned consolidation.

**Fix.** Define one record per logical call and per dispatched attempt for
all five call kinds, including failed, abandoned, and no-dispatch outcomes.
Pin content addresses and ordered refs for full and sent requests, templates,
schema, settings, wire adapter, derived inputs and retained source parts.
Define exactly which bytes each hash covers and how replay rebuilds them
and recalculates pressure. Keep exact outbound evidence in audit, with
content-addressed storage rather than repeated full copies. Exercise all
call kinds, both consolidation steps and restore with zero model calls.

### B08 — Window protection has a stop rule but no complete pre-dispatch contract

**Location.** DL §§3–5, 7–8 and 10. MF §§3.2–3.3, 4 and 7. MM §7.
Design brief §11. ADR 0013 D4/D7. P3 LLM decision spec §2.2 and §8.

**Problem.** The estimated decision, index and consolidation bounds omit
mandatory content or assume bounds the drafts do not impose. MM permits
1,160 episodic/belief/skill live-plus-history records alone, before
intentions, social records and unlimited commitments. The decision floor
includes current state, schema and verbatim outputs, while change-set
requests also include the prefix/current state, mechanical writes, open
commitments and full review records. The omission notice is added after
dropping content to fit. Neither schema defines the record for a stop before
dispatch, and the inherited P3 adapter still names a request-byte cap and a
different wire response limit.

**Counterexample.** A large but admitted change-set request drops all
optional entries and still cannot fit. The stated incomplete outcome has no
specified call/guard row or consolidation outcome to contain it. A
near-limit decision may fit just before the notice is inserted. A request
can also pass the new token check while the reused wire preparation rejects
it under P3's byte cap.

**Fix.** State the floor separately for decision and post-recall decision,
recall, morning, consolidation selection and change set. Include the notice
and provider framing at candidate counting time, and identify the treatment of
every input, including pending context and required mechanical data. Where
there is no complete bound, use the already chosen incomplete outcome with a
durable per-call record containing the measured floor, reserve, omitted
parts and reason. Align wire limits and reserve explicitly, and prove with
fixtures that neither the provider nor fallback is entered on this path.

### B09 — Sleep scheduling lacks a confirmed start boundary

**Location.** MF §§2, 3.1, 3.6 and MF-04–05. **World track:** EN §§3–4.1,
9.3–9.4 and EN-14. WAP §§2, 5.3 and 6.3. ADR 0012 D2–D3.

**Problem.** A chosen sleep starts in the first world tick, and its event is
committed with that tick's ending timestamp. A faint takes effect at the
ending boundary of a different phase. MF uses one `s` convention but gives
no handoff from these two timings to the confirmed start and the context
snapshot. EN §3 broadly freezes time for any outstanding model call. EN §9.3
also assigns a K-dependent check to the world loader, while WAP §6.3 keeps K
outside the world.

**Counterexample.** A turn at 100 chooses sleep for 36 ticks. The first
interval is 100→101 and the confirmed asleep packet arrives at 101. Counting
from that packet puts the close at 137, although the resident wakes at 136.
Counting a chosen intent immediately can start a call before the world
confirms sleep. A faint at 101 really starts its first unconscious interval
at 101. A wake at exactly `s + K` must close the old period.

**Fix.** Name start and end boundaries for each transition and retain the
pre-sleep snapshot until the world confirms sleep. State what happens to
failed or interrupted sleep, death before K, and wake exactly at K. Give
consolidation the WAP clock exception explicitly. Enforce the pack/agent
`faint_ticks ≥ K` combination at integration admission, outside world
settlement, while retaining the chosen values. Test a chosen sleep and a
faint side by side.

### B10 — The off-arm reset needs an executable state contract

**Location.** MM §§3.6 and 9. MF §6. DL §§4.2, 4.4, 6.3, 8.3, 9 and 11,
DL-17. ADR 0014 D2.

**Problem.** The declared reset retains the correct world data. MM broadly
bars store reads in the off arm but DL puts the current intention in that
store and displays it on every turn. There is no pre/post-boundary rule for
addressed proposals surviving in world sessions, and deliberate recall can
still lead into a model call under DL §6.3. A fallback queue that spans the
boundary has no stated memory-off treatment if it contains prior plans.

**Counterexample.** An off-arm resident sets a distinctive intention, takes a
nap and still needs it in the same period. In the synthetic K-sleep case,
use an unfinished dialogue with a gap longer than the sleep. Rebuilding
from the still-open world session or the saved store can revive the old
proposal or intention. Out-of-sight explored terrain has an authorized
world-delivered source and must survive that same reset.

**Fix.** Pin the off-arm reset over active intention, dialogue, recall,
prefix, log and any queued policy cognition, keeping audit and mechanical
records available for inspection. Preserve same-period intentions and
define the empty-recall path. Expand DL-17 with an interrupted nap control,
an exact K sleep, an unfinished dialogue, earlier terrain now out of sight,
and an inspection-only hard fact. Assert base personality, current
perception, position, inventory and the world-delivered map survive.

### B11 — Skill evidence cannot identify which perceived items and action phase it scores

**Location.** MF §1.2 and MF-03. MM §3.4. DL §4.3. WAP §§3.2, 4.1
and 5.2–5.4. **World-track dependency:** RR §5.2 and EN §11. ADR 0011
D3/D7 and ADR 0012 D1.

**Problem.** Own-action feedback carries the intent ID and phenomena, without
the items resolved at start. MF cites the decision perception but does not
specify how to reconstruct the exact visible looks for each resolved choice,
including by-kind quantities spanning multiple stacks. Watcher
packets can report the same intent at start, stop and settlement with no
phase, no slot roles and no quantities. Importing true slot values or
authoritative rule results into memory would violate the contract.
Inspection feedback also has no typed day-log representation for its
apparent `on_inspect` descriptors, undermining memory of WAP's permitted
instance tells.

**Counterexample.** The resident submits a by-kind input quantity that takes units
from separate stacks of the same archetype with different apparent looks.
The terminal feedback alone does not identify which quantities of each look
contributed. A watcher sees only the start and part of the
implement, yet the draft can increment `watched` for an “attempt” again
when that same intent ends. An inspected tell that appears in no later
`EntityView` vanishes from the decision context.

**Fix.** Define retained resident-safe look references and exact matching
over role, quantity and known fields, or explicitly mark an ambiguous
match unscorable. Give watcher counting a phase/deduplication rule that
works with the frozen packet. Preserve typed inspect feedback. If required
look or phase evidence cannot be expressed on the existing surface, file a
change request before changing WAP. Keep authoritative slot and outcome
data on the analysis side.

### B12 — EV-D1's manual rule is present, but its hop cannot always be joined

**Location.** EV §§3.1–3.2, 3.7 and 7.2, EV-02. DL §§3, 4.3 and
10. WAP §§5.3–5.4 and 9. ADR 0013 D4/D10 and ADR 0014 D2.

**Problem.** EV accepts a day-log ID in `cite`; DL declares only memory IDs.
Day-log entries specify identity, type, tick and text but no stable link
to a packet, utterance or observed intent. Outcome evidence can arrive on
boundaries with no decision. The 1,000-character world-entry rendering cap
can also cut speech after speaker and terms overhead, despite D4 saying
heard speech is verbatim. The manual `carries` criterion itself matches the
Owner amendment, including garbled or embellished retellings.

**Counterexample.** B hears two identical utterances from A at one boundary
with different IDs. The actual request contained only one after guarding.
B retells it without a long-term record. A checker can confirm common
content, but cannot prove which logged source entered the request or issue
a legal direct citation under DL's contract.

**Fix.** Authorize typed citations to delivered day-log entries and memory
revisions, resolved against the sent request. Persist stable, role-specific
links from each typed entry to the exact delivered item and its objective
counterpart, including deliveries with no turn. Preserve verbatim speech
separately from bounded rendering fields. Test identical speech, omitted
entries and a cited retelling marked `different`.

### B13 — World events lack the lineage needed for artifact and axe counts

**Location.** **World track:** EN §§4.1, 7.2–7.3 and 11. RR §§4.5 and 6.
EV §§3.1, 3.3–3.4 and 11. WAP §9. ADR 0014 D2.

**Problem.** `act_items` records selected-rule entity IDs with quantities,
not a material snapshot or lineage. It is absent for unmatched attempts.
EV needs exact attribution for the chosen grass cord, artifact makers and
non-maker uses. `process_progressed` identifies contributors to a process,
but the output event does not require an explicit link to that process's
completion. A shelter can satisfy the sleep condition without an identified
entity slot. The normalized unmatched `AttemptDescriptor` already has
ordered materials and quantities, so it can help classify unmatched tries,
provided EV names the evidence and predicate.

**Counterexample.** Two processes under the same rule complete on one tick
with different makers. The listed payloads alone need not identify which
shelter belongs to which contributors or which of two nearby shelters a
non-maker used. A split grass-cord stack can gain a new ID with no specified
lineage join to its earlier creation record. EV can therefore count the
same objective run differently.

**Fix.** Require stable event joins for reserved slot snapshots, stack
lineage and process-output provenance, and define the shelter-use witness.
Put material and authoritative rule information in analysis records only.
Use the unmatched descriptor for unmatched tries. Give EN/EV fixtures for a
split and merged artifact, concurrent process completions and two shelters
in reach.

### B14 — EV-D7 requires relevant learning evidence through the actual choice

**Location.** EV §§3.3, 3.7 and 7.2, EV-04/15/16. Decision sheet §3,
EV-D7. CT §§5.3–5.5. ADR 0014 D2.

**Problem.** EV adds the approved axe minimum across three on-arm runs and
requires a per-seed report. Its axe witness can still rely on a prior
`bind_snaps` record without confirming settlement, its binding material,
or whether the relevant item and `snaps` were visible to the learner.
Appearance in a manifest establishes availability. The draft lacks a
link between the relevant lesson and the choice, as requested by the Owner.
The general `own_failures`, `told` and `earlier_day` flags likewise use
verb-level or unrelated references to establish learning for the other
two-rule minimum. The per-seed unmatched branch permits a cord-only attempt
under a definition that says head, handle and cord were offered.

**Counterexample.** A watcher saw B start `combine` but never saw its cord
or the snap. That entry reaches A's later axe request, and analysis joins it
to B's hidden settlement. A's successful axe can count as learned without
A receiving the lesson. An earlier failed pot attempt can also qualify as
evidence of learning another `combine` rule.

**Fix.** Keep the Owner's counts. Require an outcome- and
material-specific antecedent, the relevant part of its delivered
representation, and an auditable turn-level link to choosing grass, such as
an applicable citation with a content check. Reserve causal claims for
probe evidence. For general flags, define rule-related evidence and
distinguish an earlier period from a calendar day explicitly. Define
`tried` from matched slots or a suitable unmatched signature. Add negative
fixtures for a start-only watch, hidden binding material, an unmet antecedent
and unrelated context.

### B15 — Several metrics lack a determinate judgment or recorded input

**Location.** EV §§1–4, 6 and 7.2, EV-07–09/12. DL §§3 and 10.
EN §11 and WAP §9. ADR 0014 D1–D2 and plan §4.1.

**Problem.** EV never assigns a judgment to a due-dated agreement still
pending at run end. Its “by due tick” rule conflicts with the approved
+6 meeting window. It needs a deterministic precedence when impossibility,
fulfilment and a due boundary coincide. “Forgotten intention” uses “matching”
free text without a matcher or human verdict, while contradictory-belief
counts cite a diagnostic with no recorded field or evidence rule. Time use
does not classify all awake intervals, such as a lone ground pickup or a
living actor's failed start. Decision manifests alone do not guarantee the
counter inputs or outcome data needed by the cost and context metrics.

**Counterexample.** A resident reaches a meeting at due +4 after a K sleep.
The judge can already have marked it broken at due, or can later count it as
kept toward the M15 minimum. A different agreement is due after the run end.
Neither classification is pinned, and the number of judged agreements can
change while all objective events stay identical.

**Fix.** Define observation horizons, inclusive intervals, deterministic
status precedence and the eligibility of judgments toward M15. Define a
metric-to-record mapping with a stable instance ID, join keys, handling of
missing inputs, and an exhaustive time-use mapping. Specify an objective
proxy or a retained annotation and evidence trace for semantic matches.
Verify complete per-turn telemetry, including failed/no-dispatch turns, and
define the probe comparator when the recorded choice and the sampled control
mode disagree. Use the same fixtures to compute results independently from
the recorded artifacts.

## Non-blocking findings

### N01 — The scalar-type deviation needs an explicit disposition

**Location.** MM §1, task-card deviations item 2 and ADR 0011 D2.

**Problem.** The handoff accurately discloses the boolean and null additions
to the ADR's string/integer scalar rule. The decision record provides no
explicit resolution of that literal mismatch.

**Counterexample.** A literal D2 validator rejects `needs_review: false`
or `last_used_tick: null` while the MM validator requires those values.

**Fix.** Record the approved encoding exception at freeze, or choose a
compliant encoding before generating readers. This is a serialization
decision independent of the memory behavior.

### N02 — The recall table drops two commitment cues

**Location.** DL §6.2 and DL-09. ADR 0011 D7 and ADR 0013 D5.

**Problem.** Commitment recipe recall is keyed to hearing the counterpart.
ADR 0011 D7 also names presence and an approaching due time. DL has neither
cue for silent presence or due time.

**Counterexample.** A resident meets its silent promisee shortly before the
due tick. The discovery recipe checks only beliefs and skills, so no
commitment is eligible through either cue.

**Fix.** Add the missing recipe triggers or make any narrower behavior an
explicit, approved decision. Test silent presence and a near-due turn.

### N03 — Commitment history and intention retirement need stable flags

**Location.** MM §§2, 3.5–3.6 and 7. DL §§4.4 and 13. ADR 0011 D1
and ADR 0013 D6.

**Problem.** ADR 0011 D1 calls for closed commitments to be archived. MM
protects them from deletion but defines no archive visibility rule.
History otherwise depends on a non-null `superseded_by`, while a cleared
intention is described as superseded with no successor. MF also permits
consolidation to retire intentions after the day showed they were dropped.

**Counterexample.** A resident clears an intention without setting another.
It has a null successor, so a reader using the history predicate can return
the old intention as current.

**Fix.** Represent retirement and commitment archival explicitly. Keep
commitment facts durable and honor the resident's authority over its current
intention. Test clearing without replacement and retiring already inactive
intentions.

### N04 — Specify the roll-up that implements R4-C-01

**Location.** EV §§2, 4.3 and 11. DL §10. MF §7. Design brief §11.

**Problem.** EV states both required reporting grains and marks later effects
as possible. It does not define how an inspection export carries a
consolidation's exposure forward to the period it supplies, or how morning
and decision guard records enter the period aggregate.

**Counterexample.** One consolidation leaves out a lesson and commits. The
entire following period has unguarded decisions. A dashboard that groups
only by a guard record's own period reports the following period as
unexposed, even though EV §4.3 calls out exactly this possibility.

**Fix.** Name separate exposure records keyed to waking period and
consolidation attempt, join them to later periods/outcomes, and retain the
guard call IDs and omissions. Add this case to EV-09 along with a guarded
morning selection.

### N05 — Finish the private visible-feedback adapter contract

**Location.** DL §8.3 and DL-13. WAP §§4.1 and 11.2. **World-track
interface:** CT §§4.3–4.5, 5.1 and 9.1. ADR 0013 D7, DL-D10.

**Problem.** The inherited compatibility phenomena support the listed
mappings for ordinary cases. The draft still points at the “matching
compatibility rule,” which risks looking up the selected rule. It also
reuses adapters whose joined-talk result and ended-action triggers are
