## Blocking findings

### B01. Exhaustion ends a tick late and can shift the completion shuffle

- **Location.** EN §§4.2, 5.1, 9.2 and 10.1, WAP §4.4 and §11.3, ADR 0009 D1.7, v1 rules §4.3 and `world/engine.py` `_resolve_boundary`.
- **Problem.** EN detects stamina exhaustion only during progress. V1 also stops an unfinished movement or gather after that tick's metabolism, before applying soft interruptions. EN additionally exempts phase-2 stops from WAP's priority across hard stops at one boundary. An unfinished action exhausted by metabolism must give way to death or fainting when the higher-priority stop applies. A phase-2 blocked step that v1 already settled before death needs an explicit treatment under WAP, whose WAP-04 asks for priority tests across pairs.
- **Counterexample.** Execute the v1 boundary fixture with Ella moving toward a distant tile at stamina 1 and Ryan waiting. In v1 Ella moves, reaches zero and ends `failure/EXHAUSTED` at tick 1. At tick 2 Ella can start six-tick rest and Ryan's wait is the sole completion. EN leaves the move active at tick 1. With no replacement turn, its late stop joins Ryan's wait at tick 2. Using the fixture's seed, one completion leaves state `20261108`, whereas two consume one draw to `559195841`. A competing completion later in the run can therefore have a different winner.
- **Fix.** Detect post-metabolism exhaustion of still unfinished moves and qualifying rules in phase 6. Project the v1 result and trigger before the next decision, and keep those stops out of the next shuffle. Specify how phase-2 stops interact with later same-boundary stops, preserving v1 calculation and WAP priority or recording a deliberate compatibility exception through the change-request process. Add the two-tick control and same-boundary death and completed-move controls.

### B02. Sleep recovery includes an extra point on each threshold boundary

- **Location.** EN §§9.1–9.2 and EN-12, CT §4.1, v1 rules §3 and `world/physiology.py` `apply_metabolism`.
- **Problem.** The draft awards recovery when both conditions are at least their minimums. V1 requires both to be strictly greater after metabolism.
- **Counterexample.** Start a sleeping tick at health 9000, satiety 2525 and hydration 2600. Both needs end at 2500. V1 leaves health at 9000. EN raises it to 9015. Holding either need alone at 2500 produces the same disagreement. With both at 2501, v1 verified recovery to 9015.
- **Fix.** Require strict comparison at the configured recovery thresholds. Pin equality on each axis, one-above on both, death after deprivation, and a final sleep tick that still gets sleep recovery.

### B03. Sleeping sight suppression changes the compatibility policy's input

- **Location.** EN §§9.1, 9.4 and 10.2, WAP §§5.1, 5.3, 6.3 and 11.2–11.3, v1 `world/observation.py` `finish_observation_tick` and `world/engine.py` `advance_tick`.
- **Problem.** The v1 boundary scan runs for every living resident, even during sleep. WAP switches the resident's senses off. Copying the v1 scan into compatibility perception would violate the frozen boundary. Suppressing the scan or its receipts can change the v1-equivalent policy inputs and later authorization. Ending a sleep in phase 3 also needs an explicit awareness distinction between utterance emission and the packet at the waking boundary.
- **Counterexample.** In a v1 engine probe Ella sleeps next to Ryan, who dies this tick. At the still-asleep boundary Ella receives `NEW_SIGHT` and a receipt for `corpse@ryan`. V2 cannot expose the corpse through the sleeping packet. If the corpse is first reported on waking, the sight and receipt arrive later, and the policy can receive a different trigger set. V1 separately suppresses hearing throughout the final sleep tick, even when the sleep completes before the later talk in the shuffle.
- **Fix.** Pin both fixtures. Decide through the frozen protocol's change-request process whether the compatibility claim excludes sleeping sight or explicitly accounts for the legacy observation and receipt state. Preserve speech filtering at emission on natural and early wake. Keep the starter pack's senses-off behavior.

### B04. The compatibility code table and supported-profile bindings are incomplete

- **Location.** CT §§1, 4.2–4.6 and CT-03, RR §2.6, WAP §§4.2–4.3 and 11.2–11.3, `world/actions.py` `validate`, and `world/resources.py` `regrow_fruit`.
- **Problem.** `compat_sleep_cave` has a condition named `cave`. Its row claims no conditions and omits the key that CT-03 and WAP-25 require for every condition. V1 has no failed-cave result because distance changes the bonus and plain sleep still succeeds. The default numeric profiles otherwise match as listed below, but CT pins one file while WAP and WAP-27 allow supported fixtures with other durations. Capacity, descriptor cutoffs, and the v1 partial regrowth increment also need a binding wherever those fields vary in a supported profile.
- **Counterexample.** A strict loader rejects the stated default code table before playing a tick. For a valid WAP-27 fixture, `give_ticks = 3` must be effective despite the production default pin. If additional v1 values are admitted, a tree at stock 4 and cap 5 with regrowth increment 2 regrows by one in v1. A literal one-unit rule matches this case accidentally and diverges from stock 3, where v1 adds two.
- **Fix.** Specify a checked unreachable-case disposition for `(compat_sleep_cave, cave)`, or use a representation that cannot expose an unmatched cave predicate as a failed v1 sleep. Since WAP requires coverage, resolve the representation or table contract through the change-request route if needed. Specify how supported fixture configs reach the compat loader and which values are template substitutions. Test profile admission/refusal and table completeness, including the configured invalid-attempt time.

### B05. Instance tells cannot be expressed by the property model

- **Location.** RR §§4.3–4.4, 7 and RR-06, WAP §5.2 and PR-D3, ADR 0008 D2.
- **Problem.** RR lets instances override apparent values only. True scale and boolean values come entirely from the archetype, material or default. A tell must be a true value different from the ordinary instance value, with the ordinary value defined by the same archetype and material. A material or archetype declaration applies to all instances using that pair.
- **Counterexample.** RR-06 requires a dry deceptive grass instance of a material whose ordinary grass is damp. A true `dry` value on the material or archetype also makes the ordinary instance dry. An apparent `dry` override cannot serve as its own tell. The positive fixture has no legal encoding.
- **Fix.** Define a validated way for the placed instance to have a genuine visible or inspectable distinction, with explicit true-value precedence and comparison against the ordinary baseline. Preserve material identity, tell visibility, and the distinction between true instance state and apparent overrides. Add a positive fixture with two instances using the same ordinary baseline.

### B06. The closed format cannot encode the whole starter pack

- **Location.** RR §§2.2, 3.2–3.4, 5.1, 5.7–5.8 and 7, CT §§1, 2, 4.2 and 5.5–5.7, WAP §3.2.
- **Problem.** Several starter rows depend on syntax or precedence that the declared format leaves unresolved. A target rule slot is either an entity predicate or a tile terrain predicate. Actor-facing verbs can take no target at all or only the declared kinds, so the targetless spring rule and the instrument-based drink rules do fit. The missing encodings affect settlement operations, the vocabulary and pack inheritance.
- **Counterexample.** The canteen and clay pot must add one serving when one capacity unit is free. `to_capacity` names a permitted quantity and a static bound, with no runtime definition of how many units to create. CT also omits `quantity` on whole-subject consumes such as `fruit_rots`'s destruction and `fire_goes_out`, and uses `subject.quantity` for the replacement stack after the subject is consumed. The output needs the pre-effect quantity and destination. The campfire and new process have no material, and several other outputs rely on archetype defaults. RR lists `material` or `material_from` as creation fields without a default/null convention. RR requires two words for every boolean descriptor while CT leaves `on_use` tables empty. Starter `gather` changes the inherited `unmatched` default to `finds_nothing`, but the extension permits adding only entries with new IDs, and the entry shape has no ID. `bind_snaps` keeps two inputs using `consumed = false` while the frozen intent describes inputs as consumed.
- **Fix.** Provide normative encodings of the affected rows, including whole-subject consumption, pre-effect reference capture, free-slot filling, default materials, `on_use` tables and one lawful comparison-table override mechanism. Clarify the consumption guarantee with WAP or express the preserved head and handle through its non-consumed instrument slots. Validate representative serialized rules as part of CT-04.

### B07. The conservation proof can be bypassed and must be branch-aware

- **Location.** RR §§3.1, 3.4, 5.4, 7 and 8, EN §§5.2 and 8, CT §5.6, ADR 0008 D7.
- **Problem.** A counted store represents real matter. `set_state` may change its non-static count, while the conservation inequality prices only explicit outputs and consumes. The inequality also needs to run for each distinct settlement path, including interrupts, and against a nonoverlapping debit of instances. Target choices are outside WAP's distinctness rule for instruments and inputs.
- **Counterexample.** A `when:` rule can add 1 to a fruit store's count with no output and no source. Alternate it with a stock withdrawal and the pair creates fruit indefinitely. A rule with one consumed unit and a two-unit partial output could also appear to conserve matter if the validator credits a consume effect that runs only on success. If a held entity is both a target consumed by an effect and the resolved input, crediting both destroys one item on paper twice. In `fruit_rots`, conserving a stack of quantity `q` depends on debiting the entire original `q` once before creating `q` replacement units.
- **Fix.** Allow matter-bearing counts to change only through ledgered operations. Validate and commit an atomic debit/transfer/credit ledger for each success, failure and interruption branch, aggregating every output against its applicable source bound. Define the rate bound for source use in `when:` rules and for shared sources across outputs. Add fixtures for store-count growth, double references, partial settlement, and multiquantities. This preserves the ADR's conservation proof while retaining declared sources.

### B08. World and process slots and heterogeneous choices lack deterministic bindings

- **Location.** RR §§4.3, 4.5, 5.2–5.3, 5.8, 6 and 7, EN §§5.2 and 7, WAP §3.2.
- **Problem.** Verb-slot permutation order is defined for intent entries. A by-kind entry may resolve across stacks with different materials. A world rule has no submitted entry order at all. The draft also permits a slot and a condition to share names such as `stock` within one rule, which can make `failed_check` ambiguous. Completion says the whole rule is revalidated without normatively ordering target, instrument slots, input slots and conditions.
- **Counterexample.** An `act` asks for three units of a single archetype from two stacks with different materials. All three can meet the slot predicate while `material_from` has two possible values. A pot-baking fire can hold an unfired pot of a selected material, and a shelter or synthetic process can contain several eligible stacks for overlapping slots. RR gives no no-reuse, tie-break, or first-complete assignment rule over contents. A rule with a missing input and a failing condition could report either of two checks on restore or in another implementation.
- **Fix.** Define ordered unit allocation from contents, backtracking for overlapping candidates, no double use, and a homogeneous-material constraint or an explicit material-selection rule for `material_from`. Preserve distinct parts of by-kind entries. Reserve a shared `failed_check` namespace and state the total revalidation order, including target and arity checks. Use identical bindings for effects, validation and replay.

### B09. The recorded context loses predicates that live matching can use

- **Location.** RR §§3.2–3.3, 6 and RR-14–15, WAP §9, ADR 0007 D9.5.
- **Problem.** `Signature` carries no set of component IDs, and `nearby_terrain` stores only the nearest tile of each kind. The matcher supports component predicates and terrain distance intervals whose lower endpoint may exclude the nearest tile.
- **Counterexample.** A rule requires grass at exactly distance 1. Attempt A stands on grass with grass also adjacent. Attempt B stands on grass with no grass adjacent. Both record nearest grass at distance 0, although the live existential test differs. A `has_component = "shelter"` test can also distinguish a marker component with no state fields, which cannot be reconstructed from the prescribed signature alone.
- **Fix.** Capture sorted component IDs and enough terrain data to reproduce every permitted interval, such as the set of observed distances per terrain in the condition radius. Define the signature for each member of a heterogeneous entry from B08 and use the same context evaluation live and in back-tests. Pin a nearest-tile-excluded fixture and a marker-component fixture.

### B10. The need-rate checker and chance sequencing leave a recovery loophole

- **Location.** RR §§5.4–5.6 and 7, EN §§4.2 and 5.2–5.3, ADR 0008 D3, WAP §4.1.
- **Problem.** The validator must combine per-tick deltas and completion deltas in one bound per sign and need. Checking a per-tick effect directly and a completion effect separately would miss the combined rate. A chance rule can also apply need recovery before rolling and later revalidate a condition changed by its own progress. RR says ordinary effects apply on success only, while EN applies per-tick effects before the outcome is known.
- **Counterexample.** With a four-tick rule and a stamina gain limit of 220, `+220` per tick and `+880` on completion pass separate comparisons but total 1760, or 440 per tick. A zero-chance rule with per-tick `+220` can raise stamina in EN and still settle `chance_failed`, contrary to the stated scope of chance-failure effects. A rest-like skill with a completion check requiring stamina below full can also invalidate itself merely by doing its own permitted work.
- **Fix.** Check `work_ticks × sum(per_tick) + sum(completion) ≤ work_ticks × limit` per sign at the minimum allowed duration, without flooring. State when progress effects remain on an unmet or chance-failed action. One compatible restriction is to disallow per-tick deltas for chance-bearing rules and document when mutable need checks are sampled. Add aggregate, clamped and self-invalidating fixtures under RR-05 and EN-07.

### B11. Process completion and interruption need a single scheduling rule

- **Location.** EN §§3–5 and 7–8, RR §§2.2 and 5.4, 5.8, WAP §§4.4 and 6.2, ADR 0007 D6 and ADR 0009 D2–D3.
- **Problem.** EN §7.3 selects the most specific eligible process rule per process, yet §8 applies every eligible world rule in rule-ID order against current state. The draft gives no scheduling point for selecting among process candidates that encounter a world change. A process with several possible work totals has no single required total for the progress descriptor. The interrupt modes also assume reserved inputs still exist, fit the new process and can be moved, including when the interrupt is triggered by death after inventory drops. For speech riding `continue`, §5.1 must say whether an old action finishing that tick makes the utterance attached.
- **Counterexample.** For one subject, two process rules are both ready. `a_finish` is less specific than `z_finish`, and `m_change` clears a condition of `z_finish` between them. Rule-ID order and a per-process specificity selection can produce different outputs. On `keep_progress` at death, the actor's reserved materials may already be on the ground or merged with an existing stack by phase 5. On a `continue` with a due old action, the two plausible readings add either one or two members to the shuffle.
- **Fix.** Define one traversal and the state used to select process rules, with one chosen rule or explicit work-total projection. Specify an atomic interrupt result when reservations have changed, the actor dies, the process is full or a placement hits a limit. Pin attachment to the action named by the current turn or explicitly make every `continue` utterance lone. Add fixtures for competing process rules and for `continue` on a due tick.

### B12. Entity limits and the ID format fail to bound all entity-producing paths

- **Location.** EN §§1, 6, 7, 11–12, CT §3, RR §§3.3–3.4 and 4.5, WAP §§3.2, 4.1 and 4.3.
- **Problem.** A transfer may split a stack and allocate an entity. Exempting every kernel transfer and death drop means the stated caps are not hard bounds on snapshot size. Rule moves out of stores, reserved splits, interruption outputs and process creation need explicit accounting. A fixed four-digit creation ordinal can overflow even with few live entities. The specified `world_limit` result is outside WAP's item-slot-or-condition wording unless it is made a real implicit condition or approved in that protocol.
- **Counterexample.** Have 16 unequal stacks on a tile and drop part of a held stack as a 17th. A living resident can repeat a split across successive ticks. An arbitrary valid rule library can use zero-matter marker entities and alternate creation and consumption more than 10,000 times within one tick while keeping the live total small. A failed capacity check after inputs are consumed or wear applied can lose material or change needs despite returning `unmet`. Two portable containers with mutually accepting predicates can also create a cycle by dropping a held container into one of its own descendants, leaving holder-tile lookup undefined.
- **Fix.** Define the counted set, prospective net change after merges, placement and holder semantics, and whether the bounds are hard or include a quantified reserved allowance for mandatory drops. Preflight every branch atomically before spending inputs or changing needs. Reject containment cycles and specify numeric overflow for both ID fields or an encoding with sufficient range. Define a protocol-compatible limit check and test the exact cap, split, merge, death, interruption and overflow paths.

### B13. The replay schema omits authoritative continuation data

- **Location.** EN §§1–3, 8, 11, 13–14, RR §§1 and 5.9, WAP §§5.3, 9 and 12, ADR 0009 D4–D5.
- **Problem.** The snapshot list never explicitly includes pending deliveries and acknowledgements, deferred stop feedback, or the v2 proposal/reply index. The replay log for a live rule addition carries its ID, hash and provenance, but no canonical rule data or specified immutable hash-to-content lookup. EN §11 also emits a smaller `place` union than the rule state supports if stores need to be identified.
- **Counterexample.** Save after phase 7 with replacement-stop feedback waiting to be acknowledged. Rebuilding it from current state can lose the old stop or redeliver it. Save before a first reply to an earlier proposal and restore with a missing reply index, or restore after a refusal and accept a second reply. Replay with only the starting pack and `rule_added` cannot reconstruct an added recipe's effects from its hash. More simply, a `source` output into a fruit-tree store increments a count, while `entity_created` and `place` cannot give it a real entity ID and a store destination.
- **Fix.** Specify the authoritative world and sim checkpoint schema, with immutable pending packets, cursors and any state needed to restore dialogue validation. Pin added rule bytes or a verified content-addressed artifact and define what restore uses at each library hash. Give store movements and source refills count-based fact shapes. Cover save-before-acknowledgement and exact replay across rule addition, store refill and reply.

### B14. Watcher-visible evidence cannot reliably identify the materials used

- **Location.** WAP §§5.1 and 5.4, EN §§10.2 and 11, CT §§5.3 and 5.5, [memory formation](../../spec/memory-formation-v0.1.md) §1.2, [decision loop](../../spec/decision-loop-v0.1.md) §4, [evaluation](../../spec/evaluation-v0.1.md) §§3.1, 3.3 and 3.4.
- **Problem.** V1 sight lists ground objects, not another resident's inventory. WAP projects `visible_items` only if each item appears in the watcher's views. EN supplies no additional lawful view of held action items and no frozen observation snapshot for consumed items. `act_items` names reserved entities only, so direct target consumption, tile actions, merged or consumed inputs, and material provenance need an explicit join over event history. An objective slot record cannot be used to fill in details the watcher never saw.
- **Counterexample.** A resident twists vine, then snaps the held vine cord while trying to make an axe. A watcher receives `snaps` with no visible cord before the attempt and no cord left at settlement. The memory skill matcher cannot match a watched vine-cord look from that record. The same issue applies to recognizing a successful grass-cord axe whose components are held and then consumed. A transferred artifact used as the target rather than an instrument or input can also be omitted if the target is not recorded as a slot.
- **Fix.** Specify what an observer can see at action start and settlement, and how recorded apparent descriptors remain attached to the observation that teaches the lesson. If workbench exposure requires widening WAP's projection, use a change request. Define objective item and material joins for evaluation, with an explicit projectable target slot, and keep hidden values out of memory. Run an end-to-end watched vine snap and a watched grass-cord success from the actual delivered packets.

### B15. The paired control needs an executable shared input and metric contract

- **Location.** CT §§9.1–9.4 and CT-13, [decision loop](../../spec/decision-loop-v0.1.md) §8.3 and DL-13, WAP §11.2, ADR 0010 D3, [P2 experiments](../../spec/p2-experiments-v0.1.md) §§3 and 5.
- **Problem.** Holding the visible-feedback policy constant across arms is a valid pack comparison. The decision loop only gives a visible-feedback fallback mode for the starter pack and otherwise selects the bridge on compatibility. It maps acts by the successful rule's phenomenon list without specifying a dispatch from the policy's visible information and its own submitted action. It also omits how to reconstruct a failed talk from its separate visible utterance feedback, and how the same mapped stop and trigger semantics are preserved. CT leaves the denominator and missing first-spring-drink handling unspecified for the tolerance and omits the P2 technical-validity condition.
- **Counterexample.** If the ordinary compatibility path is used for the control, a failed talk discards the v1 queue through the joined result. A visible-only treatment that reads only the wait's result keeps the queue, so the arms differ in policy behavior. A treatment may also record just one first spring drink at tick 8 while its control records 60 at tick 8. A median over non-null values alone passes the drinking tolerance without detecting the access collapse.
- **Fix.** Run a pinned visible-only mode in both arms, with an explicit own-action plus public-feedback table, the same running-queue behavior, and no read of authoritative settlement or the bridge. Include successful and failed speech, hard stops, urgent thresholds and contested actions. First calibrate the visible-only compatibility control against the bridge-backed equivalence fixtures. Specify 40 valid runs per round, pair and report missing drink transitions by seed and resident, and define how they enter the approved timing gate. Keep the approved tolerances and report access denominators.

### B16. The learned grass-cord acceptance test can pass on incidental context

- **Owner and location.** Evaluation §3.3, §7.2 and EV-15, with input from decision-loop trace recording. This finding belongs to the other review track. It implements the owner's EV-D7 amendment in the decision sheet §3.
- **Problem.** For the grass-cord requirement, the draft considers a relevant prior island event present anywhere in the adopted turn manifest sufficient. Presence is evidence of exposure; the owner asked for evidence tying the island lesson to the choice of grass cord. This applies separately from B14's visibility and recording issue.
- **Counterexample.** The manifest includes a watched vine snap. The adoption record gives an unrelated reason for choosing grass, or no indication that the lesson was used. The trace links event, delivery, manifest and settlement and would pass the current definition.
- **Fix.** Keep the event and exposure checks and require a traceable connection to the selected grass input, such as a cited matching context entry with a human check of the decision evidence. Add a negative fixture with a related event in context and an unrelated adopted choice, alongside the first-try and wrong-recipe controls.

## Non-blocking findings

### N01. Timestamped public IDs disclose fruit age

- **Location.** EN §1, RR §§3.1 and 4.5, CT §§2.1 and 5.6 and CT-D7, WAP §5.1.
- **Problem.** CT-D7 removes the freshness property. A public engine ID includes its creation tick.
- **Counterexample.** A fruit stack created in tick 7 and observed in tick 438 without a split or merge exposes the age of its oldest unit from `e:0000000007:...` and the current tick. The recipes can technically omit an age descriptor and still disclose this timing.
- **Fix.** Keep the authoritative timestamp private if age is intended to be learnable only from experience. Use stable opaque projected handles or a deterministic ID format without a readable timestamp. Pin a no-age-inference regression for untouched stacks.

### N02. Need polarity and actor progress lack edge-case definitions

- **Location.** RR §§2.1–2.2, EN §9.3, CT §9.3, WAP §§5.1, 5.3 and 6.3, memory formation §2.
- **Problem.** For a higher-is-worse need with a nonzero lower bound, `c = max − v` yields `0..(max−min)`. RR generally checks thresholds against the stored range. RR also supplies a progress word for process entities, while WAP can report progress changes for personal running actions. EN requires a loader to check the faint duration against K without naming a loader outside the world. WAP and memory formation keep K agent-side.
- **Counterexample.** On range 10–20, condition value 0 is real but a threshold there could be refused. A six-tick personal craft without a process has no stated rule for the tick that changes the visible progress word. Default packs use zero lower bounds. A composed run with K = 36 and a starter pack with a 35-tick faint should be refused without making the world content loader import a memory parameter.
- **Fix.** Declare the threshold domain on the normalized condition axis and define progress using elapsed work over chosen duration for timed personal actions. State the display for pending invalid attempts and actions without a fixed duration. Put the K check in composition or the run harness.

### N03. Chance and fire tests need boundary vectors

- **Location.** EN §5.3 and EN-06, CT §§5.6 and 5.9 and CT-08.
- **Problem.** Every published SHA-256 vector accepts at `k = 0`. The canonical bytes should explicitly say that the record is hashed without its JSONL terminator. Fire timing is expressed as fixed hours even though baking occurs at a global periodic boundary and a newly created fire can burn in its creation tick.
- **Counterexample.** For seed 1, tick 1, rule `a`, subject `b:3158505`, `u_0 = 4294967265` is rejected, and `u_1 = 2458963507` gives 507. Appending LF to the first published vector gives 976841585 instead of 1756018325. In a fire that already holds a pot at tick 5, baking runs at tick 6. At tick 6 with one fuel left, `bake_pot` runs before `fire_burns` and `fire_goes_out`.
- **Fix.** Record the rejection and equality-at-chance vectors, and pin ignition, insertion and last-fuel ticks. Describe baking as the next eligible global multiple of six, and state whether the ignition tick counts toward fuel consumption. Compare chance rolls across runs at identical full identities, since another action can change a later turn's intent ID.

### N04. The memory record format needs an explicit ADR variance

- **Owner and location.** [Memory model](../../spec/memory-model-v0.1.md) §1 and ADR 0011 D2. This finding belongs to the other review track.
- **Problem.** The task-card deviation is explicit and practical, but D2 limits values to strings or integers. The draft adds booleans and nulls for record fields.
- **Counterexample.** `needs_review = true` and `about_tick = null` are valid under the draft and outside the ADR's literal value types.
- **Fix.** Record the allowed typed-schema exception in the ADR acceptance trail or encode those fields with the ADR's permitted types. Keep null and false semantics distinguishable.

### N05. A successful transfer needs the frozen event ordering and fields

- **Location.** EN §§6 and 11, WAP §9, ADR 0008 D6, [evaluation](../../spec/evaluation-v0.1.md) §§3.4–3.5.
- **Problem.** EN's event table omits `action_succeeded` for pickup and drop, though the frozen event says it is emitted when a kernel action completes. Its transfer descriptions also leave stack lineage and attribution open for some operations.
- **Counterexample.** A successful tile pickup taking portions from multiple stacks could log per-stack transfers and a merge but no terminal success event under the EN table. An evaluator rebuilding makers or return-item results needs the transfer ID and pre/post owner values for each affected item.
- **Fix.** State the full event sequence for all successful kernel actions and the order of merges and transfers. Use the frozen payload, including both owner values. Add a successful multitake fixture with stable event and ID ordering.

V1 orders progress and clamped rest/sleep gains before completion, then regrowth, then metabolism and death, then sights, terminal boundary checks and commit. For a resting resident at 9950 the gain clamps to 10000 and awake decay leaves 9960. A v1 final sleep tick still counts as sleep. The v1 gather deduction runs after awake decay and any movement cost. EN moves it before those deductions. With no intervening stamina gain on the same actor's gather tick and lower-bound clipping, the resulting stamina is the same. For instance, from 320, v1 computes 320 → 280 → 0, EN computes 320 → 20 → 0. The deduction amounts and their intermediate timing differ. The v1 projection excludes those cost facts.

The v1 shuffle includes every due action and phase-2 pending failure, including invalid-at-start actions and talk. It sorts by actor ID then intent ID. The compatibility substitution contributes one one-tick wait per talk, including failed speech, so the normal completion list has the same cardinality and actor order. Xorshift32 Fisher–Yates takes zero draws for size 0 or 1, and one accepted draw per index for larger sets plus any draws rejected outside the divisible domain. The chance stream takes none from it. Preserving the completion list and initial state preserves even the rejection count. B01 gives a concrete list divergence. Phase-6 deaths and soft interrupts must not reenter the shuffle.

The inherited rules respect the stated need caps when interpreted with the aggregate check in B10. Eat is +2000 over 1 tick, either drink is +6000 over 1, rest is +120 per tick, sleep +180/+220 per tick, and gather's 300 loss over 2 is exactly the 150 limit. The only starter loss rates are 50 and 75, both below 150. Base physiology has its own order and caps.

Verb slots are assigned to exact counts and types, then ranked by the count of target and item predicate keys plus the number of conditions. A tile terrain key contributes one, quantities and declaration order contribute zero. Rule-ID byte order resolves equal scores.
